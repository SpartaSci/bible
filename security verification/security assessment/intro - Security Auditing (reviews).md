### **Security Auditing/Reviews**

**Security auditing** is a **static analysis technique** involving formal meetings aimed at evaluating a system's security, identifying vulnerabilities, and proposing fixes. This approach is particularly effective for systems and software developed in a structured manner.

Key features of security auditing include:

- **Manual Process**: While audits are primarily manual, they can be supported by automated tools. The outcomes from these tools are often discussed in the context of the manual review.
  
- **White-box Technique**: Auditors have full access to all information about the product, allowing for a thorough examination. In contrast, a **grey-box** approach is typically utilized only for certification audits conducted by third parties.

- **Development Stage Versatility**: Audits can occur at various stages of development, including **policy reviews**, **design reviews**, and **code reviews**. This flexibility enables the identification of vulnerabilities early in the development process.

- **Independent Reviewers**: Audits are usually performed by independent auditors or review teams, often in collaboration with developers. This external perspective can help identify vulnerabilities that may be overlooked by those intimately familiar with the code. Typically, a group of auditors works together, rather than relying on a single individual.

The auditing process generally follows a sequence of meetings, which may vary depending on the complexity of the program being evaluated. Before the execution phase of the audit (the actual analysis of the product), several preliminary steps are taken:

1. **Goal Definition and Planning**: Clear goals are established to determine which vulnerabilities to prioritize and the number of people involved in the audit.

2. **Preparation**: This phase may involve running analysis tools on the product to generate reports, which can be time-consuming but is essential for a comprehensive review.

3. **Execution Phase**: The actual analysis is conducted based on the predefined goals, and auditors examine the product in detail.

4. **Reporting**: After the execution, detailed reports are generated to summarize findings and recommendations, and these results are shared with the relevant stakeholders.

This structured approach ensures that security vulnerabilities are systematically identified and addressed, contributing to the overall security posture of the system or software being reviewed.
