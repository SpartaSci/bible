

**Article 1: Subject-matter and objectives**
1. This Regulation lays down rules relating to the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of [[cyber law/data protection/_key notions#Identified or Identifiable Natural Person|personal data]] and rules relating to the free movement of personal data.
2. This Regulation protects fundamental rights and freedoms of natural persons and in particular their right to the protection of personal data.
3. The free movement of personal data within the Union shall be neither restricted nor prohibited for reasons connected with the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data.


> **Article 1 of the GDPR establishes the key purpose of the regulation**, which is to set rules for the protection of individuals' personal data and to ensure their fundamental rights, particularly the right to data protection. It also emphasizes that the free movement of personal data within the European Union should not be restricted or prohibited, as long as it aligns with the protection of personal data. Thus, it aims to balance privacy rights with the need for data to flow freely for economic and operational purposes across the EU.




**Article 2: Material scope**

1. This Regulation applies to the [[cyber law/data protection/_key notions#Data processing (Article 4 GDPR)|processing]] of personal data wholly or partly by automated means and to the processing other than by automated means of personal data which form part of a filing system or are intended to form part of a filing system. **Protects personal data from both automated and non-automated processing**


This Regulation **does not apply** to the processing of personal data:
- by competent authorities for the purposes of the prevention, investigation, detection or prosecution of criminal offences or the execution of criminal penalties, including the safeguarding against and the prevention of threats to public security.(which are instead regulated by a specific directive, EU Directive 2016/680).
- in the course of Activities outside the scope of EU law (military intelligence service)
- by a natural person in the course of a purely personal or household activity;

>**Article 2 of the GDPR limits the regulation's scope** by excluding certain areas like law enforcement, military activities, and personal or household data use. These exclusions help clarify where the GDPR applies, ensuring it focuses on commercial and organizational data processing while leaving specific areas governed by other laws or outside regulation.



**Article 3: Territorial scope**

1. (**Controller in Union**) This Regulation applies to the processing of personal data in the context of the activities of an establishment of a controller or a processor in the Union, regardless of whether the processing takes place in the Union or not. 
2. (**Controller outside Union, but using Individuals in Union**) This Regulation applies to the processing of personal data of data subjects who are in the Union by a controller or processor not established in the Union, where the processing activities are related to:
    1. the offering of goods or services, irrespective of whether a payment of the data subject is required, to such data subjects in the Union; or
    2. the monitoring of their behaviour as far as their behaviour takes place within the Union.

3. (**Controller not in Union but under International public law**) This Regulation applies to the processing of personal data by a controller not established in the Union, but in a place where Member State law applies by virtue of public international law.
