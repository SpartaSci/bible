### Personal Data

- **Definition**:  
    Personal data refers to **any information** that relates to an **identified** or **identifiable** natural person. This includes not only direct identifiers like a name, identification number, or location data, but also indirect identifiers, such as IP addresses or data that, when combined, can lead to identifying an individual.
    
- **Difference from Privacy**:  
    Personal data protection extends beyond the traditional concept of **privacy**, which is typically confined to the **private sphere** of an individual’s life. Personal data includes **public and private** information about individuals, whether it concerns professional, social, or public activities.
    
his distinction was clarified in the **ECtHR case Amann v. Switzerland (No. 27798/95, 2000)**, where the court ruled that **personal data protection is not limited to private life** but encompasses any data about an individual, regardless of the context in which it is used or collected (para. 65). This expanded the understanding of how data is protected in both personal and public spheres.




### GDPR Definition (Article 4)

Under **Article 4 of the GDPR**, personal data is defined as:

- **Any information** relating to an identified or [[cyber law/data protection/Identified or Identifiable Natural Person|identifiable natural person]] (referred to as the **data subject**).
- An identifiable person is one who can be identified, **directly or indirectly**, particularly through **identifiers** such as:
    - **Name**
    - **Identification number**
    - **Location data**
    - **Online identifier**
    - **Factors specific to physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity**.

This broad definition emphasizes that even indirect information, when combined with other data, can lead to the identification of an individual, extending data protection rights to a wide range of personal information.




**IP Address as Personal Data**:  
In the **CJEU case C-70/10, Scarlet Extended v SABAM**, the court confirmed that an **IP address** can be considered **personal data**. Even though an IP address does not directly identify a person, it can be used, especially in combination with other data, to trace and identify the individual using the device. This means that IP addresses, even if they do not reveal a person's name, are still subject to data protection regulations because they can link back to an identifiable person.
