Different legal system (civil law, common law, ect)
- **Civil Law**: A legal system *based on written statutes* or codes (such as the _Napoleonic Code_). Courts interpret these laws but are generally not bound by prior judicial decisions (precedents). This system is widely used in Europe, Latin America, and parts of Asia and Africa.
    
- **Common Law**: A legal system primarily *based on judicial decisions or precedents set by courts (case law)*. While statutes exist, much of the law evolves through court rulings, with higher court decisions binding lower courts. It is prevalent in the UK, the US, Canada, and other former British colonies.


Variety of regulatory centres, National governments, international org, etc


Legal Rules: binding nature and law enforcement
- **Binding Nature**: *Legal rules are binding on individuals and institutions*. They require compliance and set standards for acceptable behavior. Failure to comply typically results in legal penalties such as fines, imprisonment, or other sanctions.
    
- **Law Enforcement**: The enforcement of legal rules *is carried out by various institutions*, including police forces, regulatory bodies, and courts. Enforcement ensures that laws are followed, and justice is served through legal penalties or corrective measures.



Nature of legal rules: general and abstract 
- **General**: Legal rules are general in nature, meaning *they apply to a broad range of people or situations rather than being limited to specific individuals or events*. For example, laws prohibiting theft apply to everyone, not just one person or case.
    
- **Abstract**: Legal rules are often formulated in *abstract terms to cover a variety of scenarios.* This allows flexibility in application across different circumstances, ensuring that the law remains adaptable to changing societal conditions. *Courts and authorities interpret these rules to apply them to specific situations.*
