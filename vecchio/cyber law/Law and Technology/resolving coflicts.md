To resolve conflicts in law, several principles and a hierarchy of courts are applied to determine which law or ruling takes precedence. These include the **hierarchy of courts**, **lex posterior** (later law prevails), and **lex specialis** (specific law prevails over general law).

---

### 1. **Hierarchy of Courts: ECJ, Constitutional Courts, and Tribunals**

- **European Court of Justice (ECJ)**:
    
    - **Highest authority on EU law**: The ECJ has ultimate jurisdiction over matters concerning **EU law**. It ensures uniform application of EU law across member states and can overrule national courts, including constitutional courts, on issues of EU law.
    - **Supremacy of EU Law**: When EU law conflicts with national law, the **principle of EU law supremacy** means that the ECJ’s interpretation must prevail over national law, including rulings by national constitutional courts.
- **Constitutional Courts**:
    
    - **Highest authority on national constitutional matters**: National constitutional courts decide whether laws comply with the national constitution. These courts have final authority on purely domestic constitutional matters.
    - **Conflict with ECJ**: If a conflict arises between national constitutional law and EU law, the **ECJ’s rulings** on EU law should take precedence under the supremacy of EU law. However, some constitutional courts have challenged this supremacy, leading to **jurisdictional tensions**.
- **Tribunals**:
    
    - **Lower-tier specialized courts**: These courts deal with specific areas like labor law, administrative law, or taxation. They are subject to review by higher national courts (including constitutional courts) and, in cases involving EU law, by the ECJ.

**Resolving Conflicts**: When a legal issue involves both national law and EU law, **EU law** takes precedence if it falls within the EU’s competencies. In matters purely concerning national law, constitutional courts are the final authority.

---

### 2. **Lex Posterior (Later Law Prevails Over Earlier Law)**

- **Definition**: The principle of **lex posterior derogat priori** states that when two laws of the **same level** (e.g., two statutes or two regulations) conflict, the **later law prevails** and effectively **repeals** the earlier law.
    
- **Application**:
    
    - This principle applies when both laws are of **equal rank** (e.g., two parliamentary statutes). If a newer law contradicts an older one, the later law is considered the valid one.
    - **Example**: If a statute passed in 2018 contradicts another statute passed in 2023, the 2023 statute overrides the 2018 one under the lex posterior principle, even if the older law has not been formally repealed.

**Resolving Conflicts**: When two laws conflict and are of the same legal rank, the more **recent law** prevails unless the newer law explicitly states otherwise.

---

### 3. **Lex Specialis (Specific Law Prevails Over General Law)**

- **Definition**: The principle of **lex specialis derogat legi generali** means that when a general law and a specific law are in conflict, the **specific law** prevails over the general law.
    
- **Application**:
    
    - If a **general law** applies to a broad range of cases and a **specific law** addresses a particular issue, the specific law governs that issue.
    - **Example**: If there is a general law on environmental protection and a specific law on water pollution, the water pollution law would take precedence in cases involving water contamination.

**Resolving Conflicts**: In situations where both a **general law** and a **specific law** could apply, the **specific law** takes precedence, ensuring that specialized situations are handled with appropriate legal frameworks.

---

### **Summary of Conflict Resolution Principles**:

1. **Hierarchy of Courts**:
    
    - **ECJ** has ultimate authority over EU law, which takes precedence over national law, including decisions of constitutional courts.
    - **Constitutional Courts** have the final say on purely national constitutional matters.
    - **Tribunals** are lower courts and must defer to higher courts on legal questions.
2. **Lex Posterior**:
    
    - When two laws of the same level conflict, the **later law** prevails over the earlier one.
3. **Lex Specialis**:
    
    - When a general law and a specific law conflict, the **specific law** prevails over the general law.

Together, these principles and hierarchies ensure that conflicts between laws and courts are resolved logically and consistently.
