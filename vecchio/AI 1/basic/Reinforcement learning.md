**Reinforcement Learning (RL)** is based on the idea of an agent performing a certain action to maximize a long-term reward concerning a specific task.

RL has the following **advantages**: 
- **learn from experience**: the trial-and-error techniques lead to improved decisions without the ground truth;
- **solve complex problems dynamically**: if the environment changes frequently, RL can potentially adapt even for complex situations;
- **continuous improvements**: new strategies are discovered over time;
- **high autonomy**: decisions are performed autonomously.

The **disadvantages** are:
- **computational cost**: RL requires a lot of data and computational power;
- **reward design**: choosing the wrong reward policies can lead to unexpected behaviours;
- **slow learning**: RL requires a lot of interactions with the environment to reach convergence in its model (and it is not even guaranteed to reach a global minimum);
- **interpretability**: the agent’s choices are not easily interpretable.
