**Digital evidence** are data that can be stored, used, or transmitted in digital form and that can be used in court of justice 




>The **digital evidence** is any information of evidential value whether memorized or sent in a digital format.

## Characteristics of Digital Evidence

The digital evidence:
- is invisible to the untrained eye;
- needs to be interpreted by a specialist;
- can be altered or destroyed through normal use;
- can be copied without limits.

## Legal Requirements of Digital Evidence

A digital evidence must be:
- **Admissible**: compliant with law and best practices; evidence can be inadmissible if obtained by violating the rights of the owner, for example.
- **Authentic**: any tampering with digital evidence needs to be avoided; otherwise, the evidence becomes unauthentic and unusable by a judge.
- **Reliable and Believable**: readily understandable for a judge.
- **Proportional**: respects fundamental rights of parties affected by the measure.

## Sources of Digital Evidence

Digital evidence can be found on:
- All devices, personal or commercial.
- The cloud, from which some evidences can be extracted.

Finding evidence can be difficult because many tools and mechanisms can be used to hide evidence.

## Types of Digital Evidence

There are three types of digital evidence:

1. **Created by man**: any piece of digital data resulting from an action taken by a human person.  
   It can be of two types:
   - **Human to human** (e.g., email).
   - **Human to PC** (e.g., Word document).

2. **Created independently by the computer**: digital data generated by a software processing data according to a specific algorithm without human intervention.  
   Examples:
   - Logs (e.g., Internet Service Provider logs).
   - **Example case**: A professor in Verona was wrongly accused based on an error in a log file. The professor committed suicide, but it was later discovered that the log file was incorrect, proving his innocence.

3. **Created by both man and the computer**: digital data where input is provided by a human, and the computer processes it.  
   Example: an electronic spreadsheet.  
   *Note*: This category is less significant compared to the first two.

## Complexity of Digital Evidence

One of the principal characteristics of digital evidence is its complexity.

### Example: The Amero Case

- **Who**: Julie Amero, a supply teacher at Kelly School in Norwich.
- **What happened**:
  - Pop-ups showing inappropriate pictures appeared during a lesson.
  - A teacher noticed pornographic files in the cache and informed the headmaster.
  - The police examined the hard drive without making a bit-stream copy.
  - The court found Julie Amero guilty of showing pornography to children.
- **Outcome**:
  - The defense team proved that:
    - Evidence was not acquired correctly.
    - The computer was infected with adware (mousetrapping), causing the pop-ups.
  - Julie Amero was fined only $100.
- **Lesson**:
  - Always collect evidence properly (e.g., take a bit-stream copy).
  - Evidence analysis is crucial for ensuring fairness.
