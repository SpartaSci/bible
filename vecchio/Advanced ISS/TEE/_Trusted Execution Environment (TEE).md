The idea of being **trusted** means that someone or something is relied upon to not compromise your security. This trust is based on human judgment—essentially, a personal belief like, "I think you can be trusted." It is about **how** you decide to use that person or system, with trust being placed in it based on experience, reputation, or expectation.

In contrast, **trustworthy** refers to the inherent capability of someone or something to guarantee that it will not compromise security. This is less about judgment and more about the actual design and behavior, focusing on **whether** the system is objectively safe to use.

A **Trusted Execution Environment (TEE)** is a secure area within a system that you might rely on to execute sensitive tasks, such as running **Trusted Applications (TAs)**. The TEE should ideally be both **trusted** and **trustworthy**. While users may place their trust in it for handling critical operations, it is equally important that the TEE is designed to actually protect against vulnerabilities and maintain security, living up to its promised guarantees.

**trustworthy implies trusted**, not viceversa
